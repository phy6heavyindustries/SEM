# SEM TECH Economics

## Processing Cost Breakdown

Based on lab-scale testing (not yet final continuous operation):

| Metric | Cost | Notes |
|--------|------|-------|
| Mining waste liquid processing | <$5/cubic yard | 255 gallons processed, <33 kWh |
| Ore processing | $50-400/ton | Depends on ore composition |
| Membrane replacement | <$1/sq ft | DIY from water softener resin + PVC cement |
| Electricity (1 ton/day) | ~$50-400 | 300-2,200 kWh at residential rates |
| Chemical inputs | Near zero | HCl and NaClO3 generated in situ from saltwater |

## Cost Drivers

### Factors That Increase Cost
1. **High sulfide content** — consumes more energy/time to react and maintain acidity
2. **High calcium content** — interferes with leaching, requires more acid regeneration
3. **Lower target metal concentration** — requires more processing per unit of metal recovered
4. **Electrode degradation** — replacement cost over time

### Factors That Decrease Cost
1. **Scale** — efficiency improves with larger systems
2. **Feedstock optimization** — pre-screening ore to adjust chemistry
3. **Process optimization** — current density, flow rates, cycle times
4. **Membrane durability** — longer-lasting membranes reduce replacement frequency

## Comparison to Traditional Mining

| Factor | SEM TECH | Traditional Mining |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| Chemical cost | Near zero (regenerated) | Continuous purchase of acids/oxidizers |
| Waste disposal | Non-toxic, agriculture-safe | Toxic tailings, long-term liability |
| Infrastructure | 50-300 sq ft for 1 ton/day | Large footprint, heavy machinery |
| Energy | Standard residential power | Industrial power grid required |
| Environmental impact | Zero pollution | High |

## Economic Projections

### Small Scale (Lab/Pilot)
- 2KW power → grams/day
- Useful for research, prototyping, small operations

### Medium Scale
- 20KW → kg/day
- Viable for specialty metals, high-value feedstocks

### Industrial Scale
- 200KW+ → tons/day
- Full commercial deployment

## Key Insight

The primary economic advantage isn't just lower costs — it's the elimination of external chemical supply chains. In remote operations or resource-constrained environments, the cost is dominated not by energy but by logistics of acid transport and waste disposal, both of which SEM eliminates through closed-loop chemistry.
