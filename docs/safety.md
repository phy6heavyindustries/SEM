# Safety in SEM Operations

## Warning: SEM is NOT a Beginner's Technology

Salt Electro Mining involves **real chemistry with real hazards**. Do not attempt without proper training and experience in:
- Electrical engineering (high-current systems)
- Mechanical engineering (system design and sealing)
- Chemical engineering (reaction management and gas handling)

## Identified Hazards

### 1. Toxic Gas Generation
| Gas | Source | Danger |
|-----|--------|--------|
| Chlorine (Cl2) | Anode electrolysis | Respiratory damage, potentially fatal at high concentrations |
| Hydrogen (H2) | Cathode electrolysis | Explosion hazard when mixed with air |
| Metal oxide dust | Ore processing | Respiratory toxicity, heavy metal exposure |

### 2. Corrosive Chemistry
| Substance | Concentration | Effect |
|-----------|--------------|--------|
| Hydrochloric acid (HCl) | Generated in-process | Chemical burns, respiratory irritation |
| Metal chloride solutions | Leaching process | Skin corrosion, systemic toxicity |
| Sodium chlorate | Oxidizer production | Strong oxidizer, fire hazard with organics |

### 3. Electrical Hazards
- High-current electrolysis (12V, 17A+)
- Risk of short circuits in conductive solutions
- Hydrogen gas generation near electrical components

## Required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

### Mandatory
- **Respirator** - Properly rated for chlorine gas and metal dust
- **Chemical-resistant gloves** - Nitrile or better
- **Eye protection** - Safety goggles or face shield
- **Proper ventilation** - Fume hood or equivalent system design

### Recommended
- Chemical-resistant apron
- Closed-toe shoes
- Emergency eyewash station
- Fire extinguisher (Class D for metal fires)

## Critical Safety Practices

1. **Ventilation First** - Design ventilation before any electrolysis begins
2. **Never run unattended** - Monitor gas generation and electrical parameters
3. **Know your system** - Understand what gases your setup generates
4. **Glove removal technique** - Proper technique to avoid skin contact
5. **Emergency procedures** - Have a plan for gas release, fire, and chemical spills
6. **Verify before proceeding** - Test for gas leaks before starting full operation

## Experience Requirements

The SEM developer emphasizes that many enthusiasts attempt this technology **without adequate experience**. You need:

- Understanding of electrochemistry principles
- Knowledge of gas handling and ventilation design
- Electrical safety awareness
- Chemical handling protocols
- Emergency response capability

If you lack these qualifications:
1. Study electrochemistry fundamentals first
2. Practice with safe, low-current electrolysis setups
3. Learn from experienced practitioners
4. Start with small-scale, well-ventilated experiments
5. Never scale up until you fully understand the hazards
